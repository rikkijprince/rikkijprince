    <h1 class="zuflucht-title">A Café Called Zuflucht</h1>

    <p class="zuflucht-subtitle">
      <em>Zuflucht</em> is German for <strong>refuge</strong>.
      But what if refuge isn't a place?
    </p>

    <div class="zuflucht-question">
      Somewhere in the world, someone is running from their past.<br>
      Someone else is running from war.<br>
      Someone is running from guilt.<br>
      Someone is running from themselves.<br><br>
      <strong>And perhaps we all need somewhere to stop.</strong>
    </div>

    <p>
      <em>A Café Called Zuflucht</em> is a story about people who have done things
      they cannot undo, carrying memories they cannot fully trust, who nevertheless
      try to create a place where they can live without constantly running.
    </p>

    <p>
      The café is more than a setting. It becomes a metaphor for the possibility
      of refuge itself: from the past, from guilt, from surveillance, from grief,
      from identity, and sometimes even from love.
    </p>

    <h2>The question behind the story</h2>

    <p>
      Can you become a different person without erasing the person you were?
    </p>

    <p>
      Can love survive when two people know the worst things about each other?
    </p>

    <p>
      What is the difference between justice and revenge?
    </p>

    <p>
      Can a place — or another person — give you enough safety to begin again?
    </p>

    <div class="zuflucht-quote">
      “It didn’t erase people’s pain. It just gave them a place to bleed without spectacle.”
    </div>

    <p>
      <strong>Zuflucht does not make people innocent.</strong>
      It gives damaged people somewhere to exist.
    </p>

    <h2>And perhaps the deeper question</h2>

    <p>
      We are complex beings. We don't always live up to society's expectations.
      We contradict ourselves. We make choices we regret. We love people we should
      perhaps not love. We carry versions of ourselves that no longer fit.
    </p>

    <p>
      In an age when artificial intelligence is beginning to make us reconsider
      intelligence, consciousness and even what it means to be human, those questions
      become harder — and more interesting.
    </p>

    <div class="zuflucht-question">
      <strong>What does refuge mean to you?</strong><br><br>
      A place? A person? A memory? A book?<br>
      Or perhaps something you haven't found yet?
    </div>
